# Semantic adjudication notes

These notes were written after the frozen 144-message blind comparison. They clarify how to apply protocol v1. They were not preregistered. All three primary coders must reread every assigned message against these boundaries; sample agreement remains the original, pre-clarification agreement and is not an accuracy estimate.

- A concerns location, availability, removal or the act of using a public bin. Do not add A merely because a dirty bin is mentioned; add it when the message also says users cannot use the bin or asks for a location/design/quantity change.
- B requires household collection context, such as truck schedules, household drop-off, building collection or work-time conflict. A vague statement that disposal is inconvenient is insufficient to infer B.
- C includes explicit uncleaned litter, dirty surroundings, overflow, insufficient cleaning or equipment damage. General street dirt is C even if no responsible cleaner is named. Do not infer an observed overflow from a hypothetical capacity limit.
- D requires an explicit disposal act, misuse, dumping source, or explicit concern that people will litter/mix waste. Litter lying on a street alone establishes C, not its source or the act of littering. Names of materials alone do not establish D or E. Garbage dropped during collection is C; D additionally requires the message to identify a disposal/source behaviour beyond routine cleaning failure.
- F includes explicitly stated odor, pests, noise or obstruction, including a named risk or concern. Do not infer pests from dirt. Water pooling alone is not one of these burdens unless access or another stated burden is described.
- G requires a distinct institutional issue: charges/bag rules, enforcement, responsibility/governance fairness, policy explanation, inconsistent rules or an ignored complaint. Addressing a mayor, naming an administration, asking for a solution or merely requesting restored bins does not by itself add G. A why-question about missing bins remains A unless it explicitly raises a policy explanation/accountability issue beyond where the bins went. Do not duplicate ordinary B/E service requests into G.
- more requires an explicit proposal to add/restore/retain, or a clearly stated inconvenience/objection caused by scarcity/removal. Bare statements such as fewer/absent bins, without a directional request or consequence, are unspecified. Do not invent support from conversational intent.
- less requires opposition to adding/retaining or a request to reduce/remove. Quoting another person's request for more bins is not the author's support. A genuine unresolved comparison may be mixed, with unclear when the text is incomplete.
- conditional requires an explicit condition for adding/retaining. Two simultaneous requests, for example restoring bins and enforcing against household dumping, are more unless restoration is made contingent on enforcement. A request for a usable bin free from misuse does not by itself impose a condition on adding/retaining.
- Household disposal stations or paid household collection bins alone do not express a public street-bin stance. If the message also discusses current street bins without a direction, use unspecified, not more. Use none if no public-bin issue is expressed.
- Themes represent expressed problems, proposals and risks; they do not establish actual events or causes. Preserve uncertainty; never fill absent context.

The parent records a reason for every changed classification and every original blind-sample disagreement. The unchanged initial labels and independent blind labels are retained privately. Revised semantic labels remain AI judgments requiring human research validation.
